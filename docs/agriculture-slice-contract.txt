Agriculture vertical slice — proposed implementation contract

Established direction
Stage 2 makes farming and settlement central, with an isometric/top-down farm
management perspective. Discussed subjects include domestication, granaries,
irrigation, pottery, fermentation, textiles, exchange and copper. The campaign
changes gameplay across ages; later stages must not be copies of one button list.
Browser first, authored art only, no loss of existing Cave/Hearth save data.

Bounded first slice, implementation proposal rather than approved final balance
- Manage a small set of fields and allocate a finite workforce between farming,
  gathering water, gathering building materials and safe food recovery.
- Choose between grain production and a restorative crop. Trade immediate yield
  against soil recovery and water demand; keep choices visible on the field.
- Assigned farm workers sow, tend, harvest and repeat. Clicking faster must never
  increase their production. Pausing and closing the page halt the simulation.
- Build storage and irrigation improvements through resources and worker time.
  Teach shortfalls through specific status text rather than unexplained waiting.
- First slice objective: demonstrate dependable harvest and storage. This is a
  milestone inside Agriculture, not a claim that Bronze or the whole age exists.
- Depleted food, water or seed must have a normal recovery route, without deaths,
  a forced reset or premium currency. Prevent over-allocation and negative stock.
- Preserve completed Hearth data before importing a starting settlement snapshot.
  Keep stage saves independent and reject unsupported future schemas. A later
  campaign contract will specify ongoing contributions from older stages and
  how new technology automates them; do not fabricate those links silently.

Deferred full-age mechanics
Exchange/copper endpoint, fermentation/textiles, broader settlement economy,
prestige and final Bronze transition need a coherent follow-on slice and tests.
The broader accepted direction remains recorded in campaign-readiness.txt.

Acceptance criteria
1. New settlement reaches the slice objective through ordinary commands, without
   direct state boosts or requiring repeated manual harvesting.
2. Soil/crop/water choices produce distinct observable outcomes.
3. Invalid or repeated commands preserve resource and workforce accounting.
4. Save/reload matches an uninterrupted run; old stages remain intact.
5. Browser playthrough covers planting, allocation, harvest, construction,
   shortage recovery, pause, refresh and stage navigation, with screenshots.
6. No simulated-time result is presented as proof of human enjoyment or pacing.

Implementation notes for this slice
The provisional numerical balance lives in src/agriculture/engine.js. Grain
produces more food but lowers soil condition; the restorative crop produces less
food and restores soil/seed. The field order repeats automatically, and changing
its next crop never restarts the current cycle. Water and food recovery stay
available even from empty stores. Worker allocation is checked before changes.
Two harvests unlock construction; one active project reserves its materials once
and advances only with assigned builders. The granary expands grain capacity;
irrigation reduces field water demand and expands the reserve. These values are
prototype balance, not historical claims or a final pacing promise.
The separate agriculture.v1 save embeds the completed Hearth snapshot and carries
its exact population and inherited traditions. Standalone preview is explicit.
The page does not save an unaccepted preview during page exit. Unknown versions
are rejected before replacement, and imported display text is escaped.

A full farm-management browser policy passed on Spark, including fresh start,
field/worker orders, both projects, the first surplus, exact refresh/export and
non-destructive Hearth transfer. No human playtest is implied by that policy.
