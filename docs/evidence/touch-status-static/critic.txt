(tests/browser/hearth-rts-move.cjs:50–51). It selects u0 through a touch tap and

innerText alone. The longer Move planned/Move blocked status variants have no
narrow override either, although this single image measures only held rendering.

Attempted disproof and severity limits

1. The aggregate quantity is visible, so no amount is actually hidden anywhere.
   Partly true: total carried stock remains readable. It does not associate the
   amount with a selected carrier when multiple villagers hold different loads.
   The screenshot itself has one loaded actor and does not demonstrate such a
   multi-load case; the general attribution limitation follows from aggregate
   versus per-unit display. This rebuts a total-information-loss claim, not the
   narrow selected-card readability finding.

2. The inspector and button title contain the complete text.
   True and useful mitigation. The inspector is below the initial viewport, and
   title is not a continuously visible touch status surface. The screenshot and
   source do not establish a screen-reader defect, nor that scrolling to the
   inspector is impossible. They establish that visible selected-card status
   cannot be read at a glance without using another surface.

3. A full-page screenshot can exaggerate the original viewport or overlay issue.
   Correct limitation. The capture must not be treated as a native screenshot
   of everything simultaneously visible on the device. The harness fixes the
   844px viewport and CSS fixes the tray to bottom:0; the selected card's actual
   ellipsis is visible in the original raster irrespective of full-page height.
   A future acceptance capture must separately use fullPage:false and record
   viewport/scroll/geometry so the visibility claim is direct.

4. The controlled clock, old invalid-target toast or stale canvas could explain it.
   Those do not explain the card's ellipsis: the visible aggregate and full Aru
   detail agree with the held JSON, and the relevant card CSS deliberately clips.
   No separate toast-timing or canvas-frame defect is inferred from this image.

5. The tray's roughly 190px footprint is itself a demonstrated defect.
   Not established. It supplies reachable command controls and explains available
   viewport space; the narrow issue can be addressed without redesigning the tray.
   This review neither requires shrinking its targets nor opens a broad mobile
   layout, text-size or input-mechanics project.

Minimal correction boundary if authorized after disproof

Prefer a narrow selected-card presentation change: let its status use enough
lines/width, clear the portrait where needed, and keep quantity plus resource
together on a short load line. A dedicated state/load pair is reasonable if CSS
alone cannot reliably present the existing combined string. An equivalently clear
selected-load surface in the command area can satisfy the user need, but avoid
adding another large block that hides the map/cards or makes controls unreachable.

Preserve selection semantics, public orders, all reducer/custody/accounting state,
save versions and bytes, migration/backup logic, native navigation and old releases.
Keep planned versus moving versus held wording truthful; do not fix clipping by
dropping the quantity, resource or meaningful state, or by further shrinking text.
Existing inspector detail remains useful. Multiple selections must not falsely
attribute an aggregate to one named actor. No mechanics change is implied.
