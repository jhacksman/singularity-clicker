T1 — CONFIRMED, transfer acceptance and first movement can miss teleportation.
Lines 124–127 assert carryMove, retained transfer/resource/cargo, no dropoff and
unchanged storage, then first-tick cargo/transfer, then held destination. They do
not compare acceptance x/y/work with the original carrier or its exact Move order,
nor independently check the first movement distance/coordinates. I attempted to
disprove this through custody()/valid(): validCarryMove requires legal contiguous
path nodes and the last node to match the order, but explicitly permits empty path
when current x/y equal the Move destination. Thus an erroneous Move that teleports
the loaded carrier to target, sets carryMove with an empty path, and preserves
storage/cargo/marker passes acceptance; the next tick changes it to holding idle,
and the current first-tick and arrival checks also pass. The gathered-load group's
stronger coordinate assertions do not test this transfer-specific branch.
Minimal repair: compare transfer acceptance x/y/cargo/resource/work to its own
before snapshot; assert exact order, next, dropoff and intended route. Observe the
next .1s tick against an independently approved first waypoint and .3 movement
budget, preserving typed load and marker. Do not infer correctness merely from
strict admission: admission proves state shape, not an allowed transition.

T2 — CONFIRMED, transfer settlement does not prove the receiving store or travel.
Lines 132–134 issue Stop, wait for job disappearance and empty cargo, and check
aggregate production/totals/transferred. They export no immediate Stop checkpoint
and check neither the eventual receipt position nor storage.stores by store ID.
A remote credit at Stop can already satisfy until() before it advances time. A
settled state with the expected total stock and counters but this carrier's load
credited to the wrong store likewise passes: valid() only checks the sum of local
stores against stock once the transfer job is pruned. The gathered group's receipt
at lines 102–106 also lacks an arrival-edge check, though it does assert retained
cargo and return phase immediately after Stop.
Counterevidence narrowing the remedy: peers in this real seed continue legitimate
pickups/deliveries while the selected carrier moves and holds. Blanket equality of
all stores across those running intervals would be a false oracle. A small safe
repair is to wait until the other job participants settle while asserting the
selected load still holds, then snapshot local stores. At Stop acceptance, assert
the selected x/y/cargo and all stores are unchanged and the planned dropoff is the
source hearth. At physical receipt, require the selected carrier at a legal edge
of that store and assert source stock increases by exactly its held load, recipient
stock is unchanged, and all other resource/store fields remain unchanged. The
earlier peer deliveries should independently account for their destination credit.
Alternatively derive those peer deltas from initial pending/carried partitions;
do not count the selected load as new gathered output or destination transfer.

T3 — CONFIRMED, gathered movement direction is derived from the candidate route.
Line 90 checks only u.path.at(-1); lines 93–94 calculate expected movement from that
same accepted u.path[0]. A legal adjacent-node path that first visits a hearth edge
and then reaches target still satisfies validCarryMove, follows its own first-node
oracle, retains cargo throughout and reaches the expected final held state well
within the timeout. The pinned old-page control detects the historical return
phase/queued-order behavior but cannot reject a new carryMove path with a hidden
hearth detour and no receipt. No current checkpoint compares the full path to an
independent direct-route expectation.
Minimal repair: for this specifically earned northern/western source and (2,3)
target, assert a separately justified shortest route or route-length/cell budget
that excludes the hearth detour, then use its independent first waypoint for the
step assertion. An independent small grid search based on public map obstacles is
another option. Do not call the candidate engine's movePath to generate its own
expected path. Avoid a general ban on store-adjacent nodes, since other legitimate
destinations or obstacles can require those nodes.

Scope: these three additions would support the existing Move browser claims.
They do not establish full IC02 acceptance, and this review is not a Spark pass.
