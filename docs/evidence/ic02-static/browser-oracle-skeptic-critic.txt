Verdict: all three gaps confirmed. These are Medium acceptance-evidence gaps

T1 — Loaded-transfer acceptance/first tick can hide teleportation or work reset

The loaded-transfer block at lines 122–127 verifies phase, marker, cargo, type,
absent dropoff and unchanged storage at acceptance. Its first-tick assertions
check only cargo/marker plus custody(). It never compares accepted x/y/work
against the pre-command carrier or checks its first movement displacement.

Attempted rebuttal: custody() invokes valid(), whose current carryMove rules
require Move, next=null, a legal target and a coherent path. Thus the claim that
there is literally no order/path validation is too broad: some properties are
checked indirectly. However, validCarryMove explicitly permits an empty path
when x/y already equal the Move target. It has no prior-state position or work
comparison and cannot distinguish legitimate arrival from command teleportation.
A faulty transfer-only acceptance that places the carrier at target with order
Move, carryMove, path[], unchanged cargo/resource/job/storage and nonnegative work
passes those checks. First tick may hold idle; until() then succeeds immediately.
The gathered-load cases exercise another origin and do not disprove this branch.

Minimal correction: assert pre-command carrier is not already at the target;
compare x, y, work, cargo and resource exactly at acceptance; assert exact Move
target, next=null, absent dropoff and a nonempty target-bound route; compare the
next .1-second displacement to the allowed .3-cell segment budget. Keep the
transfer marker and accounting checks. A target-directed route oracle must be
independent enough to address T3, not only copied from the accepted route.

T2 — Aggregate settlement cannot prove actual recipient or physical return

At line 132 Stop is followed immediately by until(job pruned && all cargo empty).
There is no exported/asserted state between the Stop command and resumed travel.
Lines 133–134 assert unchanged gathered output, typed total conservation and a
transferred counter excluding the diverted carrier. They do not assert each
store's balance or the actor's location at its receipt.

Attempted rebuttal: custody() checks the job quantity partition and aggregate
stock equals summed store stock. Neither check links a recovered quantity to a
particular receiver. After job pruning the job itself is absent. A transition
crediting the eastern destination while classifying the diverted load as recovery
can retain all asserted totals and the expected transferred count. A remote
credit to the source at the paused Stop command can also satisfy the final checks;
until() does not require that the diverted actor spend time physically returning.
This is not a claim that current receive() does either thing.

Minimal correction: export immediately after Stop while paused; assert the
carrier remains at its held coordinates with its exact load and original marker,
phase return, idle settlement intent, a route to job.from, and no store/counter
credit yet. Assert the first return step, then observe its actual cargo-to-store
transition at an eligible source perimeter, rather than only the all-actors final
predicate. A .1-tick observation around receipt can establish the adjacency
boundary without guessing from a later pruned state. Save these checkpoints.

For final balances, derive expectations from the original seed and explicitly
assert fixture preconditions. The main-browser writer permits some shares to
remain pending at its first-pickup checkpoint; do not assume every lot was picked
up. In the single-job fixture with no unrelated productive/spending activity:
  source[resource] final = source before - job.remaining + diverted cargo;
  destination[resource] final = destination before + job.amount
    - job.delivered - job.recovered - job.cancelled - diverted cargo.
Other resources and other stores must remain unchanged. This distinguishes the
source recovery from an incorrectly credited destination while retaining the
existing gathered-output/transfer-counter assertions. Comparing against the
immediate pre-Stop snapshot can simplify the source-only receipt observation if
peer delivery completion is also explicitly established.

T3 — Gathered next-step oracle follows the implementation's own chosen path

Lines 90 and 93–95 require the final path node to equal target, then calculate
expected next displacement from u.path[0] in the accepted candidate state. The
new validator checks legal adjacent route nodes but does not require a shortest
route. These are useful speed and path-following checks, not independent proof
that the route avoids the old store detour.

Attempted rebuttal: a detour that performs a receipt would be caught by cargo and
store assertions. A different counterexample remains: carryMove follows a legal
route through a hearth-side waypoint and then continues carrying to target. It
neither deposits nor changes any accounting; the path tail and each next-step
calculation still pass, and the generous arrival timeout admits the extra travel.
An old-page negative control proves the historical return phase, but it does not
make the new candidate's self-derived route oracle independent.

Minimal correction: use an independently justified route/first-leg expectation
for this specific fresh western gathering fixture. Assert the earned coordinate
and local obstacle preconditions; target (2,3) has a monotone shortest path from
the ordinary d0 working edges, avoiding the live d0 tile. A fixture-specific
shortest-route length plus legal nodes and endpoint, or an independently specified
full route, rejects a hearth-waypoint detour. An expected first leg alone rejects
the immediate homeward leg but does not exclude a detour later in the route, so
do not overclaim it. Do not impose universal Euclidean-distance decrease: valid
routes around obstacles can initially move away from their final destination.

Recommended reporting limit after fixes: state exactly which positions, route,
physical receipt boundary and individual stores were observed. Runtime closure
requires the corrected immutable test/source and actual Spark evidence. No local
execution or source fix was performed for this review.
